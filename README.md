## Limitless Lash: the dual-sided wand does the work

This is ILIA's flagship, and the thing people actually talk about is the applicator. It has two sides: short bristles on one edge for lifting from the root, a long comb-like edge for separating and extending. The practical result is that you can do a light, fanned-out everyday lash with one pass, or layer several coats into something more dramatic without the lashes gluing together. If your problem with most mascaras is clumpy, spider-leg lashes, that comb side is the fix.

The formula is built around **beeswax, organic shea butter, arginine and biotin** — the last two being the "lash care" part of the pitch, present in the ingredient list rather than implied. ILIA claims 12-hour wear and markets it as removing with warm water, no makeup remover, and no scrubbing. It isn't technically a tubing mascara, but reviewers who've used it describe the removal experience as behaving like one.

The numbers on the product page come from two consumer perception studies:

- 42 people, immediately after use: **97% said it doesn't smudge, flake or crumble**
- 42 people, after two weeks: **100% said it lifts, separates and defines**, 95% said lashes looked longer
- 32 people, after two weeks: 91% said it nourishes and fortifies lashes

That's self-reported perception data, not a lab test — worth reading as "most people in a small panel liked how it behaved," not as a guarantee. The review volume is the more useful signal: ILIA's own product page shows roughly **7,981 reviews**, and the brand claims 25,000+ five-star ratings and 19 industry awards across its history, including a 2025 Allure Readers' Choice Award (per Ulta's listing) and an Allure Best of Beauty win back in 2019.

## Fullest Volumizing: less finesse, more lash

If Limitless is about shape, Fullest is about density. The wand here is a violin-shaped brush with flexible nylon micro-bristles, designed to catch the lash line from the base and coat everything evenly. ILIA's own founder describes the split plainly: Limitless for length and a natural look, Fullest for "full, fluffy volume."

Two details make this one more interesting than it first looks:

- **It's ophthalmologist-tested**, and ILIA states it's safe for sensitive eyes and contact lens wearers — a claim that appears on both mascaras, but is repeated more explicitly here.
- **The packaging switched to a lighter, mono-material tube.** According to ILIA, citing a life cycle assessment from Bluebird Climate, that change cuts carbon emissions by 39% and waste by 22% versus the previous packaging. Not a reason to buy mascara on its own, but it does explain why the tube feels different from older stock you might still see in stores.

The consumer study behind it (32 people) reads slightly stronger than Limitless's: **100% agreed lashes looked fuller and more voluminous, 100% said it didn't feel heavy, 97% said it didn't flake or crumble, 94% said it didn't smudge.** Again — a small self-reported panel, so treat it as direction, not proof.

Honest counterweight: on one regional Sephora storefront the aggregate score sits around **4.0 across roughly 4,400 reviews**, and the negative ones cluster on the same two complaints — occasional clumping during application and end-of-day flaking. One verified buyer on ILIA's own page rated it four stars and said it gives a great natural look but flakes by evening; another said it transfers slightly beneath the lower lash line and needs a midday touch-up.

> Neither ILIA mascara is a 10-hour waterproof formula. They're water-removable by design, which is exactly why they come off with warm water and exactly why someone with very oily lids may see a little transfer late in the day.

## What outside reviewers say

The most detailed independent test I found is Who What Wear's review of Limitless Lash. Their tester's summary is useful because it lists both sides:

**Pros** — lengthens, defines and holds a curl; nourishing ingredients; doesn't smudge or flake; available in brown; removes easily; cruelty-free.

**Cons** — not the most volumizing formula they've tried; on the pricier side for a mascara.

They also note the thing most useful for buyers with stubborn lashes: it held their curl all day, which they describe as rare. On the other side of that, a Sephora UK reviewer with very straight lashes said it adds length but doesn't hold a curl well and has a noticeable scent, and a verified ILIA buyer mentioned the smell lingering for about 30–60 minutes after application. If fragrance in eye products bothers you, that's a real consideration — Fullest is listed as fragrance-free, Limitless is not.

## FAQ

**Is ILIA mascara worth $29?**
If your priority is a formula that removes with warm water, doesn't irritate sensitive eyes and separates lashes cleanly, the price is roughly in line with other clean-beauty mascaras at Sephora. If you want maximum volume in one coat, Limitless Lash will disappoint you — that's the tradeoff reviewers consistently name, and Fullest Volumizing is the correct pick instead.

**Does it flake or smudge?**
The majority of reviews across ILIA, Sephora and Ulta say no, and the consumer studies back that up. A minority report light flaking or transfer by end of day, mostly people with oily lids or humid work environments. This is not a waterproof formula.

**Which one should I pick for short or straight lashes?**
Limitless Lash. The short-bristle side lifts from the root, which is the part straight lashes need most. Curl first with a lash curler, then use the comb side to extend — one verified reviewer even switched to using only the short side because the long side made their lashes too long.

**Does it come in brown?**
Yes — Limitless Lash is sold in brown tones alongside After Midnight (soft black). Fullest Volumizing is listed in Classic Black. Brown reads softer on fair lashes and light brows, which is why a lot of the positive reviews on that shade specifically mention how natural it looks.

**Is it safe for contact lens wearers and sensitive eyes?**
ILIA states both mascaras are safe for sensitive eyes and contact lens wearers, and Fullest Volumizing is ophthalmologist-tested. Several UK and US reviewers specifically cite their sensitive eyes as the reason they stayed with it.

**How do you remove it?**
Warm water, per ILIA's own instructions — no oil cleanser or heavy-duty remover required. Reviewers describe it coming off in soft flakes rather than smearing, which is the behavior of a water-removable film rather than a wax.

## The short version

ILIA makes two mascaras, and picking between them is easier than the packaging suggests. **Limitless Lash** is the everyday option: length, lift, clean separation, dual-sided wand, brown shade available, and the one most reviewers call a repurchase. **Fullest Volumizing** is for people who want their lashes to look thicker rather than longer, and who don't mind a formula that occasionally needs a mid-day check.

Start with a mini if you've never tried either. Buy the full size if you already know. Take the Duo if you want both wands in one purchase and it's still in stock — that's the only configuration where the price genuinely works in your favour.
